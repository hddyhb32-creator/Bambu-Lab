The project

Less machine.
More possibility.

The Tiny Version is an experimental project to rethink the desktop 3D printer at a much smaller scale. The goal: a compact enclosed CoreXY machine that still feels considered, capable, and fun to use.

It is a work in progress, shared openly as the design takes shape. Every part is an opportunity to learn how a little printer can do a lot.

The idea & what you need

Power your prints away from the wall.

The concept is to run a Bambu Lab P1S from a portable 24V battery system, so the printer can be used where mains power is not available. A USB-C connection is for charging or powering compatible accessories; the printer itself needs a suitable high-power DC-to-AC inverter or a properly engineered DC supply.

01 · 24V battery system

Choose a battery with enough capacity and continuous output for the printer and its peak loads. Battery voltage and current must match the selected power architecture.

02 · USB-C PD charger

Use a USB-C Power Delivery charger compatible with the battery’s input. USB-C is for charging the battery or small accessories, not a direct P1S power connection.

03 · Power conversion

For the P1S, plan for a correctly rated inverter and safe cables, connectors, fuse, and enclosure. Confirm the printer’s actual input and startup load before buying parts.

Useful extras

Battery state-of-charge display · spare fused cable · sturdy carry case · ventilation clearance · optional USB-C PD output for a phone or LED work light.

Planning concept only: the P1S is a high-power appliance. Do not connect a 24V battery directly to the printer or assume USB-C can power it. Have the complete system sized and checked by someone qualified before use.

Good to know

A few quick
answers.

Is this a finished printer?

No. The Tiny Version is an experimental project in development. The roadmap reflects the current direction, and specifications may change as the design is tested.

Can I build one today?

The project is still taking shape. Check the GitHub page for the latest files and updates, and join Discord to follow progress and talk with the community.

What does “70 mm” mean?

It is the current target build width, not a finalized specification. The usable build volume will be confirmed as the prototype develops.

How can I follow or contribute?

Follow the project on GitHub, join the Discord community, and share questions, ideas, and feedback as the design progresses.
